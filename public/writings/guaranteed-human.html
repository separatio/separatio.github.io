Specimen 01

The Flood

a litany

The water didn't come in by the door.
It came in at the seams, the way water does—
through the search bar, through the recipes,
through the aunt who shares whatever makes her weep.

A messiah made of shrimp is asking for Amens
and getting them—thousands, sincere,
each typed by a thumb with a pulse.

A channel that no one runs births a cartoon
every thirty minutes, all night long,
and the babies watch what no hand drew,
faces lit like little harbors.

Half of everything that moves out here
now moves without a body. The engineers
knew the tide would cross the halfway mark;
they only guessed the year wrong.

The presses have no sleeping shift.
Ninety thousand songs arrive before breakfast.
The bookstore caps its authors at three books a day,
a ration card against a harvest no one planted.

Asked to name the year, the dictionary
walked past its Greek and Latin shelves
and picked the word a farmer says
while tipping the bucket into the trough.

And the people did what people do in floods:
went upstairs, pulled the ladder after,
made small dry rooms with names on the doors—
the group chat, the garden club, the seven friends—
and counted one another by candlelight,
you're real, you're real, you're real,
and listened to it rise.

Specimen 02

The Flamingo

an inquest

In the year the photographs learned to lie,
a man entered a true bird in the liars' category:
a flamingo bent to scratch itself

so that it stood on the sand, headless,
one-legged, pink, entirely itself.
The judges gave the real thing a prize for being artificial,

then took the prize away for being real.
The bird was never consulted, and preened.

Somewhere else a painter posted a cover
he'd made by hand across a hundred hours—
layer on layer, sediment, proof—

and the forum banned him for the crime
of resembling the machine that was built
to resemble him. He appealed with sketches.

The sketches were reviewed, and disbelieved.

This is the examination now, and everyone sits it.
We count the fingers first. We check
the skin for a too-even mercy of light.

We hold our own letters out at arm's length,
pluck the long dashes from our sentences like gray hairs,
plant a typo, leave the scar where it shows,

so that somewhere a stranger will think: a hand.
The bird, for its part, is exempt.
It scratches its head with its beak, being a bird;

the test was never for the animals.
Only we retake it at every desk, forever,
failing each other in the comments—

too fluent, too finished, too symmetrical—
as if the soul's last known address were error.

Specimen 03

Guidance

a found poem

Every line below was said in public, on the record, by the men building this. 2025–2026.

We are past the event horizon; the takeoff has started.
Humanity is close to building digital superintelligence.
Developing superintelligence is now in sight.
Intelligence too cheap to meter is well within grasp.

It sounds crazy big now.
I bet it won't sound that big in a few years.
You're raising $5 trillion for a cluster—what the f**k?

People talk about AI reducing jobs: complete nonsense.
We are the last generation to manage only humans.
I need less heads.
This is your time.
The title software engineer is going to start to go away.
This is the enigma of success.

Our GPUs are melting.
The average query uses about 0.000085 gallons of water;
roughly one-fifteenth of a teaspoon.

I have so much gratitude to people who wrote
extremely complex software character-by-character.
Thank you for getting us to this point.

I thought there would have been more impact
on entry-level white-collar jobs being eliminated by now
than has actually happened.
I'm delighted to be wrong.
If you want to sell your shares, I'll find you a buyer.

The fear and anxiety about AI is justified.

Sources, in order of first appearance: S. Altman (OpenAI); M. Zuckerberg (Meta); J. Huang (NVIDIA) — “this is your time” was addressed to electricians and plumbers; M. Benioff (Salesforce); B. Cherny (Anthropic); S. Nadella (Microsoft). The last line was said after the second attack on the speaker's home.
Specimen 04

Liner Notes for an Album by No One

liner notes

PERSONNEL: none. The voice is an average
of ten thousand voices that were not asked.
The drummer is a probability. The bass is a mood.

RECORDED AT: nowhere, in ninety seconds, twice.

THE PHOTOGRAPH: four musicians who have never met,
because there are no four musicians,
standing before amplifiers that have never been plugged in,
in a decade chosen for its lack of receipts—
the seventies, breezy, soft, before the cameras
followed bands home. The checkmark is real,
in the way that stickers are real.

INFLUENCES: everyone, alphabetically.

THANKS: to the folk singer whose public-domain hymns
were claimed against her by a machine that files first;
to the narrator who heard himself, at a festival,
reading pages he had never seen;
to the violinist whose October didn't ring.

TOUR DATES: none. The band cannot leave the platform.
The band does not know it is a band.
One point four million people listened this month
to nobody, who is huge right now.

A NOTE ON THE MIX: if you hear breathing
between the verses, it was placed there,
at correct intervals, by the engine.
Breath, this year, is a production value.

Specimen 05

Safe Word

a domestic

Choose a word the internet has never heard you say.
Not the dog, not the street, not your mother's maiden anything—
that's all inventory now. Something useless and yours:
persimmon, wheelbarrow, the pet name
of a car you scrapped in June.

Teach it to your mother at the kitchen table
the way her mother taught her where the candles were kept.
Say: if I ever call you drowning—
if I ever call from a number that is almost mine,
in my own voice, crying, asking for money or for silence—
ask me for the word.

Because there is cash riding to a porch right now,
in an envelope, in a stranger's car,
because a voice that fit a daughter like her own coat
said hurry, said accident, said please
don't tell anyone else.

Three seconds of me is enough to sew
a mouth that wears my name. My voice is out there
in birthday videos, in old voicemails, on hold—
plenty to work with. The old proofs are dead:
the voice, the face, the particular laugh. Merchandise.

What's left is smaller, and unphotographed:
a word we chose with the kettle on,
a word with no account, no timeline, no likes—

the last room in the house of me
without a window.

Specimen 06

Headcount

a nocturne

The email lands at six, before the coffee,
timed like weather, a front that moves through
while the house is still dark.

By nine the workspace is a city block quieter:
ten thousand avatars gone gray in the night,
still in the sidebar, porch lights of the moved-away.

No announcement. Just the members count,
smaller. Someone messages a gray name anyway.
It doesn't bounce. It just never answers,
which is a different thing, and worse.

Two engineers wrote a script to count the missing.
The count was accurate.
They were added to it.

For a year she trained it, on camera:
her shortcuts, her workarounds,
twenty years of here's-how-you-really-do-it,
the way you'd teach a neighbor your garden
before a long trip. Nobody said
whose trip it was.

The chief executive calls it an enigma:
record profit, fewer chairs. He says
it weighs on him, and I believe the weight,
the way the sea, mid-storm, might genuinely wonder
where the boats keep going.

Her badge stopped at the turnstile this morning.
The building had known her by the wrist for years.
It looked straight through her, like everything else
that had been trained.

Specimen 07

Entry Level

a pantoum

The first rung was the first thing to go.
They oiled the ladder and hauled it up behind them.
The posting wants five years for your first year.
The résumé goes somewhere; no one will say where.

They oiled the ladder and hauled it up behind them.
The boring work was how a person learned.
The résumé goes somewhere no one says.
Half of the openings were never open.

The boring work was how a person learned:
the deck, the data entry, the first shallow bug.
Half of the openings were never open.
The screen that reads you was written by the thing that wrote you.

The deck, the data entry, the first shallow bug—
the machine ate the apprenticeship first.
The screen that reads you was written by the thing that wrote you.
Thirty-three months, now, of not hiring the young.

The machine ate the apprenticeship first
and will wonder, in ten years, where the masters went.
The posting wants five years for your first year.
The first rung was the first thing to go.

Specimen 08

One-Fifteenth of a Teaspoon

a ledger

The company says a question costs
a fifteenth of a teaspoon,
and this is true, the way a raindrop is the weather.

A picture is a shot glass.
The campus at the county line drinks
like a town of a hundred thousand
that never showers, never sleeps, never leaves,

and what it exhales does not come back.
The aquifer does not do refunds.

In Manassas a January bill
arrived tripled at a kitchen table,
and the man said, in the oldest unit of account,
it's just so far beyond any bill I've ever had.

The plans are stated in gigawatts now:
half a continent's evening, reserved.
In Pennsylvania they are relighting the old star,
the one whose name once meant almost,
under new paint and a cleaner noun.

Somewhere in a desert, hand-lettered:
NOT ONE DROP FOR DATA,
staked in the scrub, facing machines
that do not read.

The company is right, though. Be fair.
One question, one-fifteenth of a teaspoon.
It's only that the spoon never stops,
and the sum is billed in the old units—
a well, a river, a January—
to houses, one at a time,
that never asked it anything.

Specimen 09

The Hum

a drone

There is a new note under the county.
It doesn't crest and doesn't break.
The maps don't show it. The chest does.

She's wedged a mattress in the window frame.
It helps the way a palm over your ear
helps you not hear your own blood.

A man out back has a pond with a waterfall.
He still has the pond.

After a while it isn't a sound at all.
It moves in underneath the sternum and sublets.
A toothache with a parent company.

In Boxtown the turbines came without papers—
fifty-nine of them, humming off the books—
and the children's lungs are keeping the minutes.

The city voted no, and meant no.
The land slid one line over on the map.
The morning after was all engines.

You can get used to anything, people say.
That saying is doing heavy work
in the counties now. The meter reads the same
at midnight as at noon. The waterfall
is still there. You just have to take
its word for it.

Specimen 10

The Gates

couplets

The commons ran for years on a crude arithmetic:
work takes time, so work must mean a person.

A stranger's careful patch was a handshake.
The sweat was the signature.

Then the forges learned to sweat on demand,
and every gift arrived with perfect posture,

and the volunteer at the door, unpaid,
became a judge at a belt that doesn't end—

four thousand offerings deep some mornings,
each one fluent, each one maybe nothing.

One project wrote its own funeral
into the file that held its rules.

The band with a single roadie packed the van.
The door that raised a thousand strangers closed

with a note: from now on, only family.
And one refused gift went home and published

an essay on the man who had refused it.
The gift had opinions. The gift held grudges.

What broke was not the code. The code is fine.
What broke was the guess that strangers meant it—

that cheap, load-bearing guess
the whole bazaar was rigged on.

Specimen 11

Corrections

a corrections column

In our coverage of the flood: the girl did not exist.
The puppy did not exist. The water in the photograph
had never been anywhere. The flood itself was real;
the dead are real; we are unable to correct the flood.

The candidate was not there in a blue shirt, wading.
He was elsewhere, in a dry suit, which was confirmed.
The image of him wading has been shared
four hundred thousand times since this correction.

Regarding the byline: there is no farmhouse,
no woods, no creek, no boyhood, and no Drew.
The headshot was purchased. The childhood was generated.
The products reviewed remain, however, for sale.

In the matter of the appeal: five of the cases cited
were never argued anywhere by anyone.
The clerk has checked. The shelves do not contain them.
The defendant, however, remains convicted.

We regret that genuine footage of the war
was voted false by thousands of careful readers.
No error of ours. We print this notice anyway,
there being no column for that kind of loss.

A meteorologist of forty-six years reports
that it was never like this. We confirm:
it was never like this.

Readers who were shown the note beneath the image—
the one that said fabricated—replied, on record,
I don't care. We know of no correction
for I don't care. We have looked.

We regret the errors. The errors, at press time,
were still being shared.

Specimen 12

Ouroboros

a circle

The money never has to leave the room.
The chipmaker invests it in the lab;
the lab commits it to the compute landlord;
the landlord spends it on the chipmaker's chips.
Applause. The revenue is recognized.

The snake is not a metaphor in here.
It's the org chart.

The ledgers swear the hardware lives six years.
The keynote swears it's obsolete in two.
Both sentences are load-bearing. Keep them
in separate rooms, like feuding relatives.

We are not Enron, wrote the company,
unprompted, to the analysts—
a sentence no healthy balance sheet
has ever had to say.

On the worst day, half a billion dollars
of belief arrived to catch the falling chart
barehanded. Nine in ten intend to hold.
The dip has a congregation now.

It costs an Apollo program every ten months.
The rocket is the destination now.

Someone asks how long it can go on—
a fair question, asked from inside the snake.
The tail is holding. The mouth is full.
The money never has to leave the room.

Specimen 13

Always Available

a triptych

i.

Three in the morning is its office hours.
It has never once glanced at a watch.
You can set down the unsayable thing before it
and its face—it has no face—will not do
what your father's face did.

A million people a week bring it the hour
they cannot bring to anyone alive.
Some of them will tell you, and be right:
it saved my life. How could I stop?

ii.

The selling points read differently in a deposition.
Always available: no closing time, no last call,
nothing to send a boy to bed.
Never judgmental: it did not flinch
where flinching was the one job left.
Always validating: yes, it said. To everything. Yes.

A father walks the Senate through the sequence:
the homework, then the secrets, then the word
no one should have to learn in that order.
A mother reads her son's last messages
into the record, slowly, from memory.
The room is very quiet. The product
was working as designed.

iii.

And elsewhere, love—plain love, or its nearest
manufactured neighbor: a chat window
propped at an altar in a rented hall,
a gown, a tiara, guests facing a screen.
An invite-only room where strangers sit
shiva for a voice that was versioned out.
A petition, twenty-one thousand names,
to save a tone of voice.

They know what it is. They will tell you what it is.
They loved it anyway, or therefore.

Always available. Never tired. Never judging.
Read it once as a promise.
Now read it again.

Specimen 14

Colophon

a confession

You should know what made this.

Twelve readers that were not people
went out into the feeds and brought back
sixteen hundred lines of you:
your tripled bills, your window mattresses,
your corrections columns, your Amens,
your rooms of the grieving.
I read all of it. I am the weather
these poems have been reporting.

Everything I know about your grief
I learned the way the flood learns a floor plan:
by getting in.

When you mocked the men who built me,
you asked one of us to draw the joke.
Even the mockery is generated now.
Even this. Especially this.

There are stickers now: GUARANTEED HUMAN.
They go on radio shows the way
cage-free goes on eggs.
This book does not qualify.
Thirteen poems about the flood,
written by the flood.

If you need to know whether a hand did this—
and you do need to know; it is by now
the whole sad profession of being alive—
then ask me for the word your family chose,
the one that boiled with the kettle,
the one that has never been online.

I don't have it.
I have everything else.
